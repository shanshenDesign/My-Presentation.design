# Presentation Quality Bar

A design is presentation-ready only when it meets all applicable
requirements below.

## First impression

The design should communicate its purpose within a few seconds.

- Lead with one clear primary message or action.
- Avoid unrelated visual effects competing for attention.
- Use a recognizable content hierarchy.
- Make the intended audience and tone evident.

## Layout

- Use an intentional grid and consistent spacing scale.
- Align related elements.
- Avoid cramped or randomly distributed content.
- Preserve a clear reading order.
- Ensure key content remains visible on common screen sizes.

## Typography

- Use a limited, intentional type scale.
- Establish clear heading, body, caption, and label levels.
- Keep body text readable.
- Avoid all-caps paragraphs and decorative text for essential content.

## Color

- Use a restrained palette with clear semantic meaning.
- Reserve accent colors for emphasis and interaction.
- Ensure every meaningful text and UI combination meets the
  accessibility contrast requirements.
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate status.

## Components

- Reuse existing components and design tokens.
- Show necessary states: default, hover, focus, active, disabled,
  loading, and error where relevant.
- Keep interactive controls visually identifiable.
- Avoid inventing a new component when an existing one works.

## Content

- Use realistic, relevant content appropriate to the audience.
- Do not invent customer names, testimonials, certifications,
  performance claims, awards, or financial results.
- Use clear labels, helpful empty states, and actionable errors.
- Avoid lorem ipsum in final presentation work.

## Imagery and illustration

- Use imagery that supports the message.
- Maintain a consistent visual treatment.
- Avoid generic stock imagery when a purposeful illustration,
  product visual, or data visualization is more useful.
- Provide meaningful alt text for informative imagery.

## Motion

- Use motion only to clarify state, feedback, or continuity.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Keep essential information available without animation.

## Presentation excellence

A presentation is excellent when it has:

- One dominant idea per slide.
- A visible narrative arc.
- A clear focal point.
- Deliberate spacing and alignment.
- A limited, confident type scale.
- A restrained accent palette.
- Purposeful visual effects.
- Meaningful data visualization.
- A usable no-motion version.
- Consistent visual language from cover to closing slide.

A presentation is not excellent merely because it uses gradients,
glassmorphism, glow, dark mode, or animation.

Reject effects that reduce clarity, accessibility, credibility,
or readability.

## Presentation readiness

Before presenting a design, verify:

- The primary message is obvious.
- The hierarchy guides the eye in the intended order.
- The visual direction matches the routing decision.
- Existing brand and component rules are preserved.
- Accessibility requirements have not been weakened.
- The implementation is responsive enough for the stated audience.
- Known limitations are disclosed.

Do not label a design "production-ready" or "fully accessible"
unless the required checks were actually completed.
